Camera name and time on the picture

Drawn over the picture as you watch. The clock is this server's, not the camera's — nothing is changed on the NVR, so moving it is always reversible.

Why the server's clock, and why it is not in an export

The server is the clock we keep correct; a camera's own burnt-in time can be minutes out without anyone noticing. On the Recordings page the overlay shows the moment being played, written the same way as the timeline underneath it.

It is drawn while you watch, so it is not in an exported MP4: exports copy the recorded video exactly as the camera made it, which is what keeps them good evidence.

Where on the picture

Held as a fraction of the picture, so it stays put on a phone, a 1080p screen and a 4K one alike.

With a secret, every message is signed: its X-Argus-Signature header is sha256= followed by the HMAC-SHA256 of the body, so the other end can tell it came from here. At most five.

Overview

    Time-lapse and retention

    The two jobs that rewrite and delete recordings on purpose, every 5 minutes while the server records: time-lapse turns footage past its full-video days into one picture every few seconds, and retention deletes footage past its total days. The days are set on the Recording tab. These two jobs keep bookmarked and exported stretches.

    The switch

      Separate from this switch, and whatever it is set to: the clean-up rules delete the oldest footage when a location runs low on space or goes over its space limit (set on each location below), and footage past a camera's total days, except bookmarked and exported stretches (a bookmark keeps the cameras it names; the automatic ones around line crossings end with their camera's total days).

      Your locations

      Folders where recordings are written. Each holds a marker file; a location without it (drive unplugged, share down) is never written and recording moves to the next one. Each can have a space limit for Argus's recordings and free-space marks of its own.

      The space limit is enforced: when Argus's recordings on a location go over it, the oldest are deleted down to it from the next clean-up (every 5 minutes, at most about 240 GB each), footage past its full-video days first; never the newest 24 hours, nor bookmarked or exported stretches. It is in GB of 1,000,000,000 bytes, at most the size of the drive or share. A limit saved before limits were enforced (29 September 2026) is not enforced until it is saved again on its card. Below a location's low mark, footage past its full-video days is deleted; below its hard floor, any footage, oldest first, and recording moves elsewhere. Empty: no limit, and the default marks (Clean-up rules, below).

      Add a folder…

      Add a network drive (NAS)

      Adds a NAS share (SMB or NFS) as a place for recordings. The whole share is mounted under /srv/cctv-net and, if you name a folder inside it, that folder is used. Test first: it mounts, writes a file, reads it back and undoes everything. The password is only ever held by the machine itself, never by this page and never in the settings file.

      Add a USB drive

      Erases a USB/eSATA drive, formats it for recordings and adds it as a location. Only drives that are not the system disk, not mounted and not in a pool are offered.

      Clean-up rules and memory

      Remote site connector

      Connects a server at an NVR site to this app over Tailscale, so the app reaches that site's NVRs by their LAN IP — no port forwarding, no fixed IP. Install it once on a Windows server that sits on the NVRs' network; it finds the LAN and joins your tailnet by itself.

      Checking for an installer…

      Download the installer

      1. Copy the download to a Windows server on the NVR site's network; if it is a .zip, unzip it, then run it (it needs admin).
      2. It installs Tailscale, works out the LAN and joins your tailnet — nothing to type.
      3. Approve the new machine's subnet route in your Tailscale admin console if it is not auto-approved.

      For the app to use these links, this server must itself be on the tailnet with subnet routes accepted. The installer is built with your tailnet key and placed on this server by an admin; it is not part of the app.

      Restart the server

      Stops and starts the Argus server. Live video and server recording pause for about 10 seconds, then every camera reconnects by itself. The NVRs keep recording to their own disks throughout. Only an admin can do this.

      Reboot the machine

      Restarts the whole Linux machine the server runs on: for when restarting the server is not enough. Live video and server recording stop for about a minute or two while it starts up again and logs back in to the NVRs. The NVRs keep recording to their own disks throughout. Not possible within 5 minutes of the last start. Only an admin can do this.

      Prepare

      Everything on this drive will be erased and cannot be recovered.
