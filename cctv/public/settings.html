Camera name and time on the picture

Drawn over the picture as you watch. The clock is this server's, not the camera's — nothing is changed on the NVR, so moving it is always reversible.

Why the server's clock, and why it is not in an export

The server is the clock we keep correct; a camera's own burnt-in time can be minutes out without anyone noticing. On the Recordings page the overlay shows the moment being played, written the same way as the timeline underneath it.

It is drawn while you watch, so it is not in an exported MP4: exports copy the recorded video exactly as the camera made it, which is what keeps them good evidence.

Where on the picture

Held as a fraction of the picture, so it stays put on a phone, a 1080p screen and a 4K one alike.

Storage locations

Folders where recordings are written. Each holds a marker file; a location without it (drive unplugged, share down) is never written and recording moves to the next one.

Network drive

Adds a NAS share (SMB or NFS) as a place for recordings. The whole share is mounted under /srv/cctv-net and, if you name a folder inside it, that folder is used. Test first: it mounts, writes a file, reads it back and undoes everything. The password is only ever held by the machine itself, never by this page and never in the settings file.

Prepare USB drive

Erases a USB/eSATA drive, formats it for recordings and adds it as a location. Only drives that are not the system disk, not mounted and not in a pool are offered.
