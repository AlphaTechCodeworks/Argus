Connect by
Type the NVR’s serial number, which is its cloud ID: it is printed on the label on the NVR and shown on its P2P page (next to the QR code) in the network settings. No address or port is needed: the server finds the NVR through the P2P cloud these NVRs are sold with (Eye in Cloud, Provision, TVT), and the serial number and the login pass through that cloud. P2P must be switched on in the NVR’s network settings. Leave P2P version on NAT 2.0 unless the NVR’s own P2P page shows it is on NAT 1.0 (nat.eyeincloud.com) — older firmware. Video is slower than on the local network, so the full-size view stays on the sub-stream.
Hikvision devices are reached at their own address on the SDK port (8000 unless it was changed on the device). Live view only for now: playback, recording and the camera settings pages are not available for Hikvision devices yet.
Connect through Hik-Connect (P2P)

Off — needs Hik-Connect developer access (AppID and server addresses from Hikvision).

Advanced: developer access from Hikvision These four come from Hikvision with developer access. Argus has no defaults for them and does not guess them; they are kept on the server and never shown again.
Unverified: Hik-Connect sign-in has not been tried against Hikvision’s service, and how the SDK reaches a recorder through it is not documented, so the device is still connected by its address.

Find NVRs on the network

Looks for TVT NVRs two ways: TVT’s own network search (UDP port 23456) and a check of every address below for an NVR answering on its SDK port. Nothing is logged in to or changed.

Networks (192.168.0.0/24, as large as /22), ranges (192.168.2.10-200) or single addresses, up to 1024 in all.

    Discover devices

    The TVT recorders and cameras on your network that are not in Argus yet, like TVT’s own search tool: each with its address, MAC, model and firmware. Anything already added is left out. The scan starts as this opens; type to narrow the list.

      Sub-streams

      Live grids play each camera’s sub-stream. Browsers on PCs without H.265 hardware decoding can only play H.264, so H.265 sub-streams stay black there. This switches the chosen cameras’ sub-stream to H.264 on the camera itself; main streams and recordings are not changed. You can undo it here.
