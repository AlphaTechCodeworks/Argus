    What this check does and does not prove

    If the check passes, it proves two things: every file in this pack is exactly as it was when the pack was signed, and they were all signed together by whoever held the signing key. Change one byte of one video file and this page will say so.

    It does not prove who signed it. Anyone can make their own signing key, build a pack of their own footage with it, and that pack will also say it passed. What proves the pack came from the recording system you think it did is the signing key itself. To be sure of that, you need the recording system's public key from somewhere other than this USB stick or download — from the system's own settings page, or from whoever ran the export, given to you separately — and then compare it with the key below.

    This pack is protected with a password

    One or more of the files you opened is an encrypted pack (its contents are scrambled until the password is supplied). This page cannot unscramble it, and does not try: getting that wrong could tell you a correct password was wrong.

    Ask whoever sent you the pack for the password. They set it when they made the export; it is not stored anywhere in these files and cannot be recovered from them. With the password, the pack can be unlocked on the recording system that produced it, or with the export tool that came with it, and the unlocked folder can then be opened here.
